THE COLLECTOR

Read a merchant's catalogue out of a page you are already looking at. No request is made to the merchant: the page was fetched because you asked for it, in your own browser, as a visitor.

Why this exists, and why it runs before the scraper.

Every merchant in this repo ships pending, and no merchant can be published without a reviewed capture committed to data/captured/. That is the reverse of how the three sister sites were built, and it is the reverse on purpose: each of them registered vendors, pointed a scraper at them, shipped, and found out afterwards. One vendor has returned zero products since the day it was added. Two others put 466 products up that earn nothing to this day. Reading the catalogue first is the cheap version of learning that.

1 · INSTALL

Drag one of these to your bookmarks bar. Then open a merchant, and click it.

GD Collect GD Collect (self-contained)

Which one?

  • GD Collect is short and always current. It loads the collector from this site each time, so a fix reaches you the next time you click it.
  • Self-contained carries the whole program in the bookmark itself. Use it on a shop whose script-src refuses the loader — and note that a blocked script fires no error, so the tell is that nothing at all happens.

Current build …. The panel prints the build it is running. If those two differ, your self-contained bookmark is stale — re-drag it. A stale collector does not throw; it returns a smaller catalogue that looks entirely plausible, which is how a shop on the sister site was reported broken months after it was fixed.

2 · CAPTURE

  • Scroll to the bottom first if the grid lazy-loads. What is in the DOM is all this can see.
  • Capture every page of a paginated grid. They merge into one record per merchant, so there is no cost to running it repeatedly.
  • Check the merchant key the panel guessed. Filing a capture under the wrong merchant is the failure that gets found by a shopper rather than by us.
  • A menu inside a cross-origin iframe cannot be read. That is the same-origin policy working correctly. Open that frame in its own tab and run it there.
  • An empty capture is refused, not stored. Running it before the grid rendered cannot wipe out earlier work.

3 · IF SENDING FAILS

A shop can permit the read and forbid the send — connect-src does that, and it surfaces as a bare TypeError with nothing to read. The collector opens a relay tab automatically, because opening a tab is a navigation rather than a connection and no policy governs it. If pop-ups are blocked, press Copy and paste below.

Typed here, used for one request, never stored. It is never in the bookmarklet URL — that lives in a bookmarks bar in plain sight forever.

4 · LOOK AT WHAT YOU CAPTURED

The same grid the storefront uses, over the raw capture. Nothing here is published — the gate is still data/captured/ — but you can see a merchant's shelf the moment you scan it, and the room shown on each card is where _scene.js would actually file it. A room that comes out obviously wrong on real titles is a pattern to fix before the merchant ships.

Draft every shop that is waiting does one request per shop that is captured but not reviewed. Triage every unread shop is the other end of the funnel: it reads the live feed of every shop nobody has looked at yet, in batches, and returns one block each — counts, how much of the catalogue is off scene, and the product types. Reading 50 full analyses is not a thing anybody does in one sitting, and a review nobody can physically read is the same as no review. Triage first, pick, then draft the ones you picked (the Merchant key box takes a comma-separated list).

Read the live feed skips the walk entirely: the server reads the shop’s own products.json and drafts the same file. 53 of the 54 merchants here are Shopify, and Shopify publishes that endpoint so that machines can read it, so for those a hand walk is ceremony rather than diligence. It still publishes nothing — reviewedBy comes back blank like every other draft. Use the bookmarklet when a feed is switched off at the edge, or when you want to see the shop the way a shopper does.

Three states, and only the third one puts products on the shelf: captured means rows are in Neon; reviewed means somebody read the catalogue and committed data/captured/<key>.json with their name in it; published also needs pending cleared for that key in api/_stores.js. Scraping a shop does the first one only. Live from /api/capture?worklist.
